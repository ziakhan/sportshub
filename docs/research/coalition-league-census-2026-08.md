# Coalition Basketball League — full census

Owner ask 2026-08-17. Pulled first-party from Coalition's own Exposure Events pages (they run everything on Exposure, org 27075) plus coalitionbasketball.com.

> **Headline: the owner's instinct was right. Coalition is the second-biggest basketball league in Ontario, and our own census badly understated it — we counted 417 summer teams and never enumerated the winter season, which carries 890.**

---

## 1. What Coalition actually is

**The Coalition Basketball League**, founded and run by **Ben Sanders** (Founder + Boys Commissioner) with **Natasha Bussoli** as Girls Commissioner. Contact: `info@coalitionbasketballleague.com`.

Structurally it is **two things at once**, which is why headline numbers conflict:

1. **A 14-club member association** — "made up of 14 teams from across southern Ontario" is their own copy, and it means 14 *member organisations*, not 14 teams.
2. **An open league operator** — their leagues accept entries from any program, and the overwhelming majority of entrants are **not** members. In Summer 2025, only ~53 of 417 entries came from member clubs; ~363 came from ~155 other programs.

**Everything runs on Exposure Events.** No proprietary platform. They also publish a mobile app (iOS + Android) and broadcast games on YouTube.

**What's included, in their own words:** *"No Administration or Registration Fees"* · *"Individual Player Insurance included for 1 year"* · *"FREE ADMISSION"* · All-Star Weekend · shot clocks for high-school games · *"Top-Quality Facilities & Athlete-Focused Amenities."*

**Early-bird incentives:** winter teams paying in full by Oct 5 get **12 Molten FIBA basketballs**; summer teams paying by June 1 get **12 SwishZone passes** (HoopDome or Scarborough). Clubs paying larger amounts can be put on a **payment schedule**.

| NPH Showcase | **$3,990** |

**Coalition's winter fee is $295 (10%) *above* OBL's U10–U14 rate and only 20% below NPH.** This is not a budget alternative — it is a premium product priced between the two.

**And the "no administration or registration fees" line is a direct shot at OBA.** OBL's $2,900 is *before* the $100 team registration fee, $40 per player, $50 per coach and manager, $500 club affiliation, and $100-per-day back-end registration penalty. **A 12-player OBL team's true all-in cost is roughly $3,580; Coalition's $3,195 includes insurance and admission.** Coalition is cheaper all-in while looking more expensive on the sticker — and they say so on the page.

### Where the Coalition app comes from (owner question 2026-08-17)

**It is Exposure's white-label product, published under SBA's developer account, talking only to Exposure's servers. ARC is not involved anywhere.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Android package** | `com.exposure.thecoalitionleague` — the `com.exposure` prefix is the giveaway |
| **iOS listing** | `apps.apple.com/ca/app/the-coalition-league/id1578748427` |
| **Listed developer** | **Scarborough Basketball Association** — SBA operates Coalition, so the app sits on SBA's Apple developer account |

| **Data source** | **Exposure only.** It renders the Coalition event data — schedule, standings, brackets, rosters, results — from Exposure's servers |

So the chain is: **Exposure builds and maintains the app → Coalition pays $299 + $99/yr to have it skinned with their logo → it publishes under SBA's Apple account → it reads Exposure's event data.** Coalition wrote no software and owns no product; they rent a skin.

### What the app actually does

Exposure's branded-app feature list, verbatim: *"Multi-Sport, Team search, View schedules, View standings, View brackets, Bracket results, Receive notifications, Venue directions, Team rosters, Live results and box scores, View documents, Sponsors, Ad banner integration, Register, Analytics."*

**It is a read-only event companion.** Schedules, standings, brackets, rosters, directions, push alerts, sponsor slots. **No chat, no RSVP, no team communication, no media, no player profiles.**

⚠️ **And "box scores" overstates it.** Their live Exposure schedule and standings pages contain **zero box-score or stat links** — the pages load division lists client-side and stop at final scores. Exposure has no native play-by-play; organisers who want stats pay separately for NBN23, HoopStats or iScore, and **Coalition pays for none of them.**

> **890 winter teams × ~11 games ≈ 5,000 games a season, and the only data captured is a final score typed in by hand.**

**ARC's own positioning is startlingly close to ours:**

> *"**Live Visual Stat Tracking** mobile app allows scorekeepers and parents to provide an **ESPN-like experience**… when scores are pushed to the ARC server, standings and schedules update… unlock **trophies**, track live scores and **cheer on everyone you follow**."*

Social graph, live stats, player careers, gamification, following. **That is our thesis, stated by a company operating in our market.**

### 🚨 The line that settles how far along they are

Printed on their own basketball page:

> **"NOTE: App access is in Beta on Android for Organizations utilizing our system"**

**Android only. Still in beta. Restricted to orgs already on their platform.** No iOS at all — which matches the App Store search returning nothing.

**So the honest competitive read, without overstating it:** ARC has a working registration and league-management business and an *ambition* identical to ours. The consumer half — the app, the live stats, the athlete pages, the following graph — is an Android beta. They are building the same thing and are behind on the part that matters.

**And note their emphasis differs from ours.** ARC leads with an **athlete website builder for recruiting exposure** — a paid product sold to the *player*. We lead with the league and club system of record, with the player identity falling out of real game data. Theirs is a résumé you fill in; ours is a record that fills itself.

**Why this matters to us:**
1. **They are the only competitor found whose stated vision matches ours** — social layer, live stats, player careers, Ontario-based.
2. **They have distribution we do not:** five Ontario basketball associations already registering through them, including the club that operates Coalition.
3. **They have not delivered the part that matters.** No app, no stats in the field, no player pages visible anywhere.
4. **Watch them.** If ARC ships its consumer layer to SBA/MUMBA/Burlington/Durham, they become the direct competitor overnight. Right now the door is open.

Five to six vendors, five bills, five logins, and the data never joins up.

### 5a-0. ✅ PROVEN: no live scoring, no stats, not even quarter scores

Owner question 2026-08-17: are they using Exposure's stats partners, or does someone type in a final score?

**Definitively answered from Exposure's own game payloads.** Every game record carries fields that would be populated if any live-scoring or statistics integration were running. Across **all 6,971 games of both seasons**:

| Field | What it would carry | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| `StatisticsType` | Names the stats provider (NBN23, HoopStats, iScore, StatCrew) | **null on 100%** |
| `LiveGameLink` | URL to a live gamecast | **null on 100%** |
| `Assets` | Attached video or media | **empty on 100%** |
| `HomeTeamOtherScores` / `AwayTeamOtherScores` | **Quarter-by-quarter scores** | **empty on 100%** |
| `Started` | Live in-progress flag | **False on 99.9%** |
| `Status` | | `Final` on 99.9% |

The six games where `Started` is True all have `Status: null` and **no score at all** — they are unplayed fixtures in late March 2026 with a stale flag, not live games.

And the Exposure game-detail page contains **no** box score, play-by-play, quarter, period, stats, points or rebound content of any kind.

> **Verdict: Coalition records exactly one number per game — the final score — entered by a human after the game. There is no live scoring, no box score, no player statistics, and not even a period-by-period breakdown. 6,971 games a year produce 6,971 integers.**

**This is the single most defensible claim in the whole pitch**, because it comes from Exposure's own API rather than from an absence on a marketing page. Exposure *supports* NBN23, HoopStats, iScore and StatCrew — Coalition has connected none of them, and the null `StatisticsType` on every game proves it.

⚠️ **Say it carefully.** This is not a failure of Exposure and it is not incompetence by Ben. Wiring a stats provider costs money and needs a trained scorekeeper at every one of up to 43 courts on a Sunday. **The honest framing is that the capability has never been within reach at their scale — not that they neglected it.**

### 5a-i. 🚨 WHAT COALITION PAYS EXPOSURE — and why it breaks our league pricing

Owner question 2026-08-17: what does a club pay to register?

**A club pays Exposure nothing.** Exposure's customer is the *organiser*, not the participant. From Exposure's published pricing: single-team registration is **free** to set up, and the registrant pays only **merchant fees** (ordinary card processing). EventStore multi-team registration adds a **1% Exposure fee** on the transaction. That is the club's entire exposure to Exposure. Everything else a club pays goes to Coalition: **$3,195 winter, $2,400 summer per team.**

**What Coalition pays Exposure to run the whole thing:**

| Line | Basis | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling credits | 1,538 team-entries × $2 | **$3,076** |
| Branded app | $299 one-time + $99/yr Apple | $99/yr |
| Marketing credits (est.) | ~10 × $30 | $300 |
| **Total, annual** | | **≈ $3,475** |

Other organiser-side options if they use them: banner advertising $250–$2,500, featured event postings $125–$350, EventStore 1%.

> **Coalition runs a ~$3.9M league on roughly $3,475 a year of software. That is 0.09% of revenue, about $2 per team.**

#### ⚠️ This is a serious problem for the league pricing in [[business-model-v3]]

| | Per year | vs Exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure costs Coalition | **$3,475** | — |
| Our proposed **$19/team** | $29,222 | **8×** |
| Our proposed **$39/team** | $59,982 | **17×** |

**We cannot walk in asking 17× the incumbent's price.** The earlier reasoning — "$39 is only 1.2% of their $3,195 team fee, so it's affordable" — is true about *affordability* and irrelevant to *comparison*. Ben will not compare our fee to his revenue. He will compare it to his software bill, which is $3,475.

**Three ways out, and the third is almost certainly right:**
1. **Match the unit** — price at $2–5/team ($3,000–7,700/yr). Competitive, but earns us almost nothing.
2. **Price on outcome** — charge against new revenue we create (sponsorship, family subscriptions), not per team. Complex, slow.
3. **⭐ Free to Coalition, monetise the clubs and the families.** This is consistent with the v3 thesis (leagues are supply, families are the profit engine), it removes the only objection he can quantify, and the 407 clubs and ~14,000 families behind him are worth far more than $60k. **The league fee is the wrong thing to fight for here.**

**And it explains why Coalition has not switched away from Exposure despite the gaps: at $2 per team, there is no cost pressure to leave.** The only lever that moves them is capability, not price.

## 6. What this means for us

**1. Coalition is the second-biggest target in Ontario and we had it mis-sized.** Winter 890 + Summer 417 makes it the clear #2 behind OBL and far ahead of NPH. It belongs alongside OBL and NPH in the pitch list, not below the National Circuit.

**2. It is the single most accessible target of the three.** No PSO politics (unlike OBA), no incumbent national partnership (unlike RAMP/Canada Basketball), one decision-maker (**Ben Sanders**), and a platform (Exposure Events) that we already know does **no live scoring, no family app, no player profiles, no recaps** — the exact gaps we fill. Coalition rents Exposure the same way NPH does.

**3. The tier ladder is the product insight.** Ten divisions deep means Coalition's real value proposition is *"every team finds its level."* That requires constant re-tiering as results come in — a scheduling and standings problem that is painful on Exposure and natural for us.

**4. The audience.** 890 winter + 417 summer team entries × ~12 players ≈ **15,700 player-seasons**, comparable to OBL's ~15,600. Allowing for the same kids playing both seasons, Coalition still reaches on the order of **8,000–10,000 distinct families**.

**5. Girls are 18%.** Across OBL (29%), Coalition winter (18%) and Coalition summer (19%), the girls' game is structurally under-served in Ontario. That is both a social argument and a growth market for whoever serves it properly.

**6. Venue overlap is the sleeper finding.** Six Park East, HoopDome, TSS Athletic Centre, GTA Sportsplex and Skillz Basketball Lab host OBL, NPH *and* Coalition. **A venue-availability product would have three paying leagues on the same inventory** — and it explains the weekend-availability crunch we found in the NPH analysis.
