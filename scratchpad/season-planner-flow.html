Proposal · operator experience

Plan your season in five steps

One guided flow that mirrors how a league operator already works: confirm what we already know, answer the one thing we can't know, get the calendar, publish, then schedule when registration locks. Every screen below uses NPH's real world. Nothing is built yet; this is the thing to react to.

  • Ask only what we can't know
  • Everything else is pre-filled — history decides, they confirm
  • Venue truth lives in one place
  • The plan is remembered — it drives every step after
  • Plan first, schedule follows

Teams

“How many teams do you expect?”

One grid, one number per grade. Prefilled from last season where history exists. No dates, no venues, no jargon — this is the estimate they already carry in their head in August.

The mind-read: the flow starts where they start every summer — a guess about teams — not where software usually starts (forms about “divisions” and “sessions”).

Gyms & weekends

“Here's your gym time — confirm it.”

Not a form. The gyms they've used are already on the card — courts, hours, and the season's weekends all selectable. Every weekend starts available: we never mark a gym unavailable for them — they untick what they don't have, because they're the ones who know. One card per gym is the whole venue model; a one-weekend exception is edited on the cell itself, never in a settings page.

The mind-read: they open this step and their gyms are already there. And if a facility shares its real calendar with us one day (Six Park's owners could), it flows straight onto this grid — until then, nothing is assumed.

Your calendar

We hand them their own graphic

No preference questions. The grouping rules are baked in because they're league truths, not choices: oldest grades together, youngest together, the middle split by size, the two biggest grades kept apart — and each grade leaning to the gym it has historically played in. The system proposes; the operator adjusts by dragging.

Grade chips drag between weekends; the court math recomputes live. Amber means tight, dashed means the gym isn't yours that weekend. A quiet “Adjust grouping rules” link exists for the operator who cares — it is never a step.

The mind-read: this screen is the calendar NPH drew by hand and posted — same five columns, same grade blocks, same gyms per grade. They'll recognize their own decisions, computed in a second.

Publish

The calendar lives here — and travels anywhere

Publishing puts the calendar on the league's own page, where clubs and families already look — on the platform, it's a living view, not a poster. The downloadable graphic exists for everywhere else: Instagram, print, email.

Publishing also freezes the plan as the season's baseline: persisted, manually editable later, and quietly carried forward as the input to registration caps, the plan-vs-actuals watch, and scheduling. For a per-session league, this same step shows prices and entry rules; the flow doesn't change shape. If teams don't exist yet, the flow simply rests here — a published calendar, waiting for sign-ups.

The mind-read: the graphic they hand-built in a design tool last year is now a byproduct of planning — and unlike their old poster, this one is still connected to everything that happens next.

Schedule

Scheduling is the follow-through, not a second product

While registration runs, this step is a watch-screen: actual sign-ups against the step-1 estimate, per grade. The moment actuals break a weekend, it says so — in October, while a court can still be booked.

When entries lock (or earlier, if they want), one button generates the schedule from everything already entered. Zero re-entry: gyms, hours, weekends, groupings all flow from steps 2–3.

The mind-read: nobody asks them for venue hours a second time. The plan is the scheduler's input — which is exactly how they think about it.

The other leagues

The whole picture, without a bigger wizard

We never ask “do you want to plan your other leagues too?” — that's a question with an obvious answer and a scary commitment. Instead: the moment one league publishes, the next one is a single tap away, and it starts with everything already known — same gyms, same grid, minus the weekends the first league just consumed.

Each league keeps its own tiny five-step plan. The org calendar is a view, not a flow: every league's weekends layered on one board. Big leagues take buildings; small ones fill leftover courts — the way NPH already runs D1 inside Six Park on Showcase weekends.

The mind-read: they plan Showcase first and the rest follows — which is exactly their real order of operations. The software never makes them choose it; it just meets them there.

Where things live afterward

After the wizard, the same five steps become the season's home page — a rail with checkmarks where every step reopens for editing in place. Gym facts stay on the step-2 card and nowhere else; the calendar stays on step 3 and re-renders the card and the league page when the plan changes. The published plan is the season's remembered baseline: registration measures itself against it, scheduling reads from it, and manual edits to it flow downstream. The wizard and the workspace are the same screens, so nothing learned during setup has to be re-found later.
